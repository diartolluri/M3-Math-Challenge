SECTION 5 — MODELLING METHOD SELECTION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Q2 has a natural two-stage structure:
  STAGE 1 — Participation & Volume Model: Given demographics, 
             what is the expected annual total amount wagered?
  STAGE 2 — Outcome Model: Given total wagered and bet type (risk 
             tolerance), what is the expected annual net gain or loss?

The elegance of this structure is that Stage 2 collapses to a simple 
expected value formula once Stage 1 is complete, and the whole model 
can be validated against the aggregate GGR figures from the data.

5a. RECOMMENDED MODEL — state the specific approach for each stage.

    For Stage 1 (Participation and Volume), three approaches:
    (A) EMPIRICAL LOOKUP: Use the provided demographic breakdown 
        of participation rates and frequency/stake distributions 
        directly, with linear interpolation for intermediate values. 
        Fast and anchored to real data.
    (B) LOGISTIC REGRESSION: Fit a logistic regression on the 
        participation data to estimate P(gambles | demographics) 
        as a continuous function. More flexible, requires more 
        statistical work.
    (C) DEMOGRAPHIC WEIGHTING: Assign each demographic group a 
        participation probability and expected annual handle from 
        the provided distributions, combining them to get an 
        expected annual amount wagered per person.
    
    For Stage 2 (Outcome), the core formula is:
    Expected Annual Net = - H × W
    where H = effective hold rate (function of risk tolerance) 
    and W = expected annual handle (total wagered, from Stage 1)
    
    Risk tolerance modifies H as follows:
    - Conservative bettor (spread/moneyline only): H ≈ 4.5-7%
    - Average bettor (mix of bet types): H ≈ 10% (implied by GGR data)
    - High-risk bettor (parlays, props, long-shots): H ≈ 15-25%+
    Quantify these hold rates with reference to published sources 
    and explain the mathematics behind why parlays carry higher 
    house edge than single-game bets.

    Additionally, consider whether the model should output ONLY 
    expected value or also a measure of variance (standard deviation 
    of annual outcome), which depends on risk tolerance. Higher risk 
    tolerance = same or worse expected value but MUCH higher variance. 
    This is mathematically important and distinguishes a sophisticated 
    solution.
